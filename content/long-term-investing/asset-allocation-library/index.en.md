## Why I Keep This List

Over the past year, I've looked at a lot of assets.

US stock ETFs, A-share ETFs, QDII funds, bonds, gold, REITs, crypto assets, active funds — there are a lot of names. Look at ticker symbols alone and it's easy to get confused.

These days I prefer to group them into a few broad categories. That makes things much clearer: some assets are a core position, some are for growth, some are for cash management, some are defensive, and some are just small opportunistic bets.

This list isn't about buying everything on it. It just puts the assets I've paid attention to in one place, so I can make trade-offs later.

## The 8 Baskets

| Basket | Representative Assets | Main Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global Broad Market | VOO, VTI, S&P 500, CSI 300, A500 | Participate in long-term market growth |
| Tech Growth | VGT, Nasdaq, STAR Market, semiconductors | Provide growth elasticity |
| Cash Flow Assets | Dividend low-volatility, REITs | Dividends, rent, infrastructure cash flow |
| Fixed Income Stabilizers | Government bonds, short-term bonds, money market funds, convertible bonds | Reduce volatility, hold short-term money |
| Inflation Hedges | Gold, commodities, nonferrous metals, copper, soybean meal | Hedge inflation and resource cycles |
| Regional Diversification | India, Japan, Europe, Southeast Asia, Hong Kong stocks | Don't bet on a single market |
| Asymmetric Opportunities | BTC, ETH, AI Crypto, Web3 | Small positions on future opportunities |
| Active Enhancement | Active funds, active tech, active growth | See whether the fund manager delivers excess returns |

This way of grouping is more useful than looking at individual holdings.

Take VGT, the Nasdaq, semiconductors, and the STAR 50: different names, but much of the time they're solving the same problem — tech growth. SGOV, YinHua RiLi, short-term financing ETFs, and short-term bond funds are all roughly solving the cash management problem too.

What I care about is the role an asset plays in the portfolio, not whether it went up recently.

## My Take

Once you own a lot of assets, the most likely problem isn't missing opportunities. It's duplicated exposure.

See a new AI ETF, and it may already overlap with VGT, the Nasdaq, and semiconductors. See a new short-term bond fund, and it may be doing the same job as SGOV, YinHua RiLi, and short-term financing ETFs. See a new crypto project, and you first need to be clear whether it's infrastructure, an application, a platform ecosystem, or just a hot name.

For me, investing isn't about owning more tickers.

For an asset to earn a place in the portfolio, I at least need to be able to explain its position. If I can't explain it, I put it aside for now.
