## How I Think About Crypto Assets

I do look at crypto assets, but I won't weight them heavily.

The reason is simple: there really is something new here — directions like stablecoins, on-chain finance, DeFi, AI agents, and global trading. But it's also very unstable. The rises and falls are both extreme, and many projects are hot today and barely mentioned a while later.

So I won't treat it as the foundation of our household assets.

For me, it's more like a small observation zone. I buy a little so I keep following the industry as it develops; I keep the position small so it doesn't affect our household cash flow or the rhythm of our life.

## What Each of These Assets Actually Does

### ETH: The Base Asset for On-Chain Applications

ETH can be understood as the base asset of the Ethereum ecosystem.

A lot of DeFi, stablecoins, NFTs, and on-chain applications are tied to Ethereum. When you transfer, trade, or call a contract on-chain, you often have to pay fees in ETH. ETH can also be staked to earn a bit of staking yield.

When I look at ETH, I'm not mainly looking at how much it can rise in the short term. I'm looking at whether on-chain applications will still exist in the future. If on-chain finance and stablecoins keep developing, ETH will be hard to bypass entirely.

Its problems are obvious too: fees, scaling, Layer 2s taking value away, and competition from other public chains will all affect how ETH performs.

### LINK: Feeding Data to On-Chain Applications

Behind LINK is Chainlink.

A blockchain doesn't know on its own what happened in the real world. How much ETH costs, how much gold costs, whether the price of some collateral has fallen below a line — all of that needs external data.

What Chainlink does is bring that data on-chain. DeFi lending, liquidations, derivatives — many places use this kind of data service.

I follow LINK because the need is fairly real. But whether the token itself can turn that need into long-term value is still something to watch.

### AAVE: The On-Chain Lending Market

AAVE is DeFi lending.

Some people deposit assets to earn interest, others pledge assets to borrow money, and smart contracts automatically enforce the rules in between. You can think of it as an on-chain money market.

It's easier to understand than a lot of pure-story projects, because people really do use it, and it really does have revenue.

But the risks aren't small either: contract vulnerabilities, collateral crashing, liquidation cascades, and regulatory changes can all affect it.

### stETH: ETH With Staking Yield

stETH is Lido's ETH staking receipt.

You stake your ETH, and Lido gives you stETH. It roughly represents the ETH you staked, and it also reflects the staking yield.

If you were planning to hold ETH for the long term anyway, stETH can be understood as a way to "earn a little extra staking yield."

But it adds a layer of risk compared with holding ETH directly: Lido protocol risk, liquidity discounts, and DeFi composability risk all have to be factored in.

### wBETH: The ETH Staking Asset Inside Binance

wBETH is similar to stETH — it's also an ETH staking-related asset.

The difference is that it depends more on the Binance ecosystem. If you mainly operate inside Binance, it's more convenient. The downside is just as direct: stronger platform dependence, and a lower degree of decentralisation.

So it's not better or worse than stETH — the use case is different.

### BNB: The Token of the Binance Ecosystem

BNB is a bit different from ETH.

ETH is more like the base asset of an open public chain, while BNB is more like the token inside the large Binance ecosystem. It's tied to the trading platform, BNB Chain, fees, Launchpad, and ecosystem applications.

BNB's strength is that it has real users and a real business ecosystem behind it. The downside is right there too: it's highly dependent on Binance, and platform risk and regulatory risk are both fairly concentrated.

So I won't treat BNB as a replacement for ETH. It's more like part of a trading-platform ecosystem.

### VIRTUAL: An Early Project in the AI Agent Direction

Behind VIRTUAL is Virtuals Protocol.

I follow it because it puts AI agents and crypto together.

If in the future AI agents aren't just chat tools, but can provide services online, charge money, split revenue, and have their own identity and wallet, blockchains might have a useful role to play.

But this direction is still very early. Real users, real revenue, how the token captures value — none of that has been fully proven yet.

So VIRTUAL is suitable for watching, not for getting carried away with.

### PAXG: Gold On-Chain

PAXG is fairly simple — it's a gold-pegged asset.

It's not a growth asset, and it shouldn't be compared with ETH, BNB, or VIRTUAL on gains. It's more like moving gold on-chain, so it's convenient to hold, transfer, and trade inside a crypto account.

Its role leans defensive, not offensive.

### MANA: A Metaverse-Themed Asset

MANA is Decentraland's token, representing directions like virtual land, virtual worlds, and a metaverse economy.

This story was very hot before, but real progress hasn't been as fast as imagined. Users, frequency of use, and commercialisation all still need to prove themselves again.

So I'll put MANA in thematic observation, and I won't treat it as a core asset.

## How I'd Position Them

| Asset | How I understand it | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ETH | The base asset for on-chain applications | Core observation within crypto |
| LINK | On-chain data services | Infrastructure observation |
| AAVE | On-chain lending market | DeFi observation |
| stETH / wBETH | ETH with staking properties | ETH enhancement tools |
| BNB | Binance ecosystem token | Platform ecosystem observation |
| VIRTUAL | AI Agent + Crypto | High-risk, small-position observation |
| PAXG | Gold on-chain | Defensive asset |
| MANA | Metaverse theme | Thematic observation |

My principles are simple:

More foundational assets like BTC and ETH can be watched over the long term at a small allocation.

Assets with actual use, like LINK, AAVE, and BNB, are worth watching to see whether they really have long-term demand.

More thematic ones like VIRTUAL and MANA call for more caution. You can listen to the story, but your position shouldn't follow the story.

And don't fantasise about PAXG surging — it's more like gold.

Crypto assets are the easiest to get emotional about, because they move so fast. For a regular family, the most important thing isn't betting on the right coin — it's not letting one wrong judgement affect your life.

The real foundation is still cash flow, a cash buffer, index funds, mortgage management, and control over household spending. Crypto assets can exist, but only as a very small layer.
